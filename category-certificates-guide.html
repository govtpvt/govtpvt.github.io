Category Certificates for Government Jobs: SC, ST, OBC-NCL, EWS and PwBD

A reserved-category claim in a government job form is only as good as the certificate behind it. Most rejections at document verification come from a certificate that is in the wrong format or dated on the wrong side of the cut-off. This guide explains what each certificate is and what verifying officers look for.

The certificates at a glance

Central list and state list are different

A community can be OBC in a state list and not in the central list. For central government recruitment, only the central list counts. State commissions follow the state list and often limit reservation to candidates domiciled in that state.

Dates matter more than anything

Notifications state the date by which the certificate must have been issued, and for OBC-NCL and EWS, the financial year it must relate to. A valid claim with a certificate from the wrong year is treated as unreserved, and if you do not meet the unreserved cut-off, the candidature ends there.

Use the format in the notification

Most notifications carry the certificate format as an annexure. Take a printout of that annexure to the issuing office so that the certificate you receive matches it.

Relaxations that come with the category

Age relaxation, fee concession and a lower qualifying mark are common, but each notification sets its own. If you use any relaxation, you are normally counted against reserved vacancies only.

Before you apply

Open the notification from the latest government jobs list on Govtpvt, find the section on reservation, and confirm three things: the list that applies, the certificate date required and the format.
